Prakash: 2:00 AM → 11:30–11:45 PM bedtime

3. Do not add a morning workout until bedtime is reliably ≤ 12:30 AM. Adding exercise before the sleep debt is repaid just stacks a second willpower demand on top of a body that's already running a deficit — this is the most common reason new gym habits collapse in week 2.

DIET BASELINE — LIPIDS & VITAMIN D

This is a starting direction based on general nutrition evidence, not a personalized medical plan — his doctor should set anything with a specific number (dose, target) attached.

Central fat + high cholesterol + high triglycerides on a lean frame

This pattern (lean body weight with visceral/central fat — sometimes called "TOFI," thin-outside-fat-inside) is one where triglycerides in particular tend to respond strongly to added sugar, refined carbohydrate, and alcohol intake, and to physical inactivity — often more than to total dietary fat. Cholesterol is more individually variable. This means the diet lever most worth pulling first is refined carbs/added sugar, not fat restriction.

The egg question

He's open to eggs if medically justified — worth knowing the current state of evidence: nutrition science has shifted over the last ~15 years toward viewing dietary cholesterol (from eggs) as having a smaller effect on blood LDL for most people than previously believed, with saturated/trans fat mattering more. That said, a meaningful minority of people are "hyperresponders" where dietary cholesterol does move their blood lipids more. Given he already has an elevated lipid panel, the sensible approach isn't a blanket yes or no — it's reintroducing eggs (if he wants the protein/vitamin D/choline benefit) and rechecking his lipid panel 8–12 weeks later to see how he personally responds, ideally with his doctor's input on the plan.

Vitamin D

Given the existing low reading, this is worth discussing directly with his doctor for a dose set from his actual lab value — a vegetarian diet without eggs is a genuinely low-vitamin-D diet by default, so food alone is unlikely to close the gap; supplementation dosed to his specific level is the standard approach.

Vegetarian omega-3

Flax, walnuts, and chia provide ALA, but the body converts ALA to the more bioactive EPA/DHA forms inefficiently. Since triglycerides are one of the values already elevated, an algae-based DHA/EPA supplement (a vegetarian-compatible source, unlike fish oil) is worth raising with his doctor as a targeted option.

THE REAL PROBLEM ISN'T COOKING TIME — IT'S UNDIVIDED DECISIONS

One hour of daily cooking isn't the issue on its own. The fix separates deciding from doing. Decide once a month (the rotation below). Do it fast, twice a week (the batch system). Assign ownership explicitly so it doesn't silently default to one person again.

ARCHITECTURE: COOK TWICE, EAT SIX TIMES

Indian home cooking is naturally modular — a grain, a legume, a vegetable, and a tempering recombine into different meals. Two batch sessions a week produce all of it:

1. Sunday, 4:00–5:30 PM — Batch Session 1. Instant Pot runs the week's dal base. Rice cooker runs a big batch of rice. Air fryer roasts a tray of vegetables or tofu/paneer. This also fills the Monday–Thursday lunch containers (see below).
2. Wednesday, 5:45–6:15 PM — Batch Session 2. A shorter refresh: a second protein or vegetable variant, timed to double as Wednesday's one-pot dinner itself, so there's no separate "cooking" on top of the day's dinner — and starting at 5:45 lands dinner at ~6:30 with the whole evening intact behind it (audit correction: the original 6:30 slot pushed dinner too late).

THE RAMP EVENTS — ONE HONEST LIMITATION

The bedtime events (Prakash's and Ahana's) sit at their end-state times, with the 15-min-every-3-4-nights ramp instructions inside the event. A calendar can't smoothly slide an event 15 minutes every few nights — no tool does this well. The pragmatic version: follow the in-event ramp instructions off your current bedtime, and let the calendar event be the finish line you're walking toward. If you want a moving cue instead, the router wifi-cutoff schedule (below) is the thing to nudge earlier twice a week — it's a 20-second edit and it's the enforcement layer anyway.

AUTOMATION INVENTORY — SET ONCE, NEVER DECIDE AGAIN

Everything below removes a recurring decision permanently. Roughly in order of payoff:

1. Router wifi schedule (Prakash's boundary): most routers (or their apps — e.g., Eero, Google Wifi, Nest, or the ISP app) support scheduled internet pauses per device. Set Prakash's laptop + phone to pause 30 min after the shutdown-ritual event. This is the single automation doing the most work in the whole system.
2. Phone Do Not Disturb / Sleep Focus schedules on both phones, aligned to each person's wind-down. Both iOS and Android support scheduled modes; allow calls from each other and the nanny through, always.
3. Robot vacuum: daily 10:30 AM run scheduled in its app (post-breakfast, pre-lunch mess).
4. Recurring subscriptions for Ahana's consumables (diapers, wipes, snacks — e.g., Amazon Subscribe & Save or Target auto-restock). Size the cadence generously; an extra box beats a 9 PM store run.
5. Autopay on every bill that supports it; card-not-bank where possible for the float and the single statement to reconcile monthly.
6. HEB app: after the first delivery order, save it as a favorite/reorder list — the Friday event assumes this and drops to ~5 minutes.
7. Medication alarms as backup redundancy: two phone alarms on Shraddha's phone labeled with the literal action ("BP pill — with lunch"), independent of the calendar. Adherence systems should be two-layer; phones get silenced, organizers get overlooked, rarely both.
8. Smart plug on a living-room lamp, scheduled to dim/turn off at Ahana's wind-down start — an environmental cue the whole family reads without anyone announcing bedtime.
9. Savings/investing: confirm every transfer (401k, brokerage, 529 if opened) is on auto-schedule — at your savings rate this is likely already true; the monthly review just confirms it fired.

NOTION — THE MANUAL, DELIBERATELY SMALL

A warning before the build, aimed with respect at a known pattern: an elaborate Notion workspace is one of the most seductive procrastination vectors available to a systems-thinker with perfectionist tendencies. Building the system starts to feel like running it. So this build is deliberately minimal — one page and four databases — and the rule is: if a Notion page hasn't been opened in a month, delete it, don't improve it.

STRUCTURE (≈45 MINUTES TO BUILD, ONCE)

1. HOME (one page, everything below nested in it): at top, the year's 2–3 priorities from the annual review. Then links to the four databases and the reference pages.
2. Database 1 — Weekly Reset Log: columns: date, outlier this week (text), dropped tasks (text), swaps decided (text). One row per Sunday, filled during the 15-minute reset. Create a template row so it's fill-in-the-blanks.
3. Database 2 — Money Reviews: columns: date, type (monthly/quarterly/annual), savings rate, decisions made, deferred to date. The "open decisions" the monthly review works through is just a filtered view of rows with a deferred date.
4. Database 3 — Brag Docs: two pages (one each), reverse-chronological bullets, fed by the Friday 10-minute event. Nothing fancier.
5. Database 4 — Nanny Notes: one row per day, 2 lines (what she played with / new words or firsts). Share the database or page link with the nanny if she's comfortable; a paper notebook she prefers is an equally correct implementation.
6. Reference pages (static, no databases): the 4-week meal rotation grid; the standing task ownership list; the four decision trees from Module 3; the screen policy one-pager; the home-buying gates with current status; the CPA question list from Module 4.

What is deliberately NOT in Notion: daily tasks, habit trackers, dashboards, progress bars. The calendar runs the days; Notion remembers things. Any feature that requires daily maintenance to stay true is a decision-generator, which is the opposite of the brief.

OPERATING MANUAL — HOW THE SYSTEM MAINTAINS ITSELF

The "another family could run the household from the manual" test, honestly assessed: the calendar events + printable checklists + Notion reference pages ARE that manual. A capable adult with access to those three layers could run a normal week cold. What no manual covers is judgment on abnormal days — which is why the decision trees exist for the four most likely abnormal days, and why the Weekly Reset exists to catch everything else within seven days.

The self-maintenance loop, stated explicitly:

1. Daily: the calendar fires; people follow events; nobody plans anything.
2. Weekly (15 min): the reset catches drift, empties the Google Tasks inbox, and adapts next week.
3. Monthly (30 min): money stays reconciled; the learning share keeps the marriage a conversation.
4. Quarterly (90 min): portfolio, promotion arcs, and the house gates get their only sanctioned airtime.
5. Annually (half-day): goals reset, and the OS itself gets reviewed — events that got ignored all year get deleted, not felt guilty about. A system that prunes itself survives; one that only accretes gets abandoned.
